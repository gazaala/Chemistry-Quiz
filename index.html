Key Definitions
1. Matter:

Anything that has mass, occupies space, and can be perceived by our senses.

2. Atomicity:

The total number of atoms present in one molecule of an element or compound.

3. Chemical Change:

A permanent and irreversible change in which one or more new substances with entirely new properties are formed.

4. Mass Number (A):

The total sum of the number of protons and neutrons (nucleons) present inside the nucleus of an atom.

5. Valency:

The combining capacity of an atom of an element with other atoms to achieve a stable electronic configuration.

6. Sublimation:

The process in which a solid directly changes into gaseous state on heating without passing through the liquid state.

7. Heterogeneous Mixture:

A mixture in which the components are not uniformly distributed throughout its volume and have visible boundaries of separation.

8. Radical (Polyatomic Ion):

A group of atoms of different elements bound together that carries an overall positive or negative charge and acts as a single unit in reactions.

Give Reasons (Conceptual)

1. Why is an atom electrically neutral?

Total positively charged protons in nucleus = total negatively charged revolving electrons.

2. Why are noble gases chemically inert?

Their valence shells are completely filled (Duplet in He, Octet in Neon/Argon).

3. Why is water a compound, not a mixture?

Combined in fixed mass ratio (1:8), properties differ from constituent H₂ & O₂ gases, cannot be separated physically.

4. Why do gases diffuse faster than liquids?

Gas particles possess high kinetic energy and large intermolecular spaces with negligible attraction.

5. Why sugar volume doesn't increase water volume?

Sugar particles occupy the existing intermolecular spaces between water molecules.
